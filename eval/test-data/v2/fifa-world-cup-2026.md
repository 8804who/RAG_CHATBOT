# 2026 FIFA World Cup

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_final
Retrieved: 2026-11-21

## Overview

The 2026 FIFA World Cup was the 23rd FIFA World Cup. It was jointly hosted by three countries, Canada, Mexico and the United States, and used 16 host cities: 11 in the United States, 3 in Mexico and 2 in Canada. The tournament began on 11 June 2026 and concluded on 19 July 2026.

The tournament featured 48 teams, drawn from all six confederations, and 104 matches. A total of 308 goals were scored, an average of 2.96 goals per match. Total attendance was 6,810,966, or 65,490 per match.

The opening match was played by Mexico on 11 June 2026 at the Estadio Azteca in Mexico City.

## Final

The final was played on 19 July 2026 at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey, in front of 80,663 spectators. The referee was Slavko Vinčić of Slovenia.

Spain beat Argentina 1–0 after extra time. The only goal was scored by Ferran Torres in the 106th minute, from a header by Nico Williams. Ferran Torres was named player of the match.

Spain's head coach was Luis de la Fuente; Argentina's head coach was Lionel Scaloni.

It was Spain's second World Cup title and its first since 2010.

## Path to the final

- **Spain** beat France 2–0 in the semi-final on 14 July in Dallas.
- **Argentina** beat England 2–1 in the semi-final on 15 July in Atlanta.

## Individual awards

| Award | Winner |
|---|---|
| Golden Boot (top scorer) | Kylian Mbappé (France), 10 goals |
| Golden Ball (best player) | Rodri (Spain) |
| Golden Glove (best goalkeeper) | Unai Simón (Spain) |
| Young Player Award | Pau Cubarsí (Spain) |
| FIFA Fair Play Award | Netherlands |

Spanish players won three of the four main individual awards: Rodri the Golden Ball, Unai Simón the Golden Glove and Pau Cubarsí the Young Player Award. France's Kylian Mbappé won the Golden Boot with 10 goals even though France finished fourth.

## Summary

Spain became world champions for the second time by beating Argentina 1–0 after extra time in the final at MetLife Stadium, with a 106th-minute goal by Ferran Torres. The expanded 48-team, 104-match tournament in Canada, Mexico and the United States produced 308 goals and an attendance of more than 6.8 million.
